Action-conditioned world model — bug log
=========================================
Scope: latent dynamics z_{t+1} = f(z_t, a_t) over object slots + robot state, and the
candidate-action scoring loop built on it. Our own code, not an adaptation, so the
Fidelity Rule's checkpoint test does not apply — the equivalent bar is that the learned
model must beat the trivial predictors on held-out data, or it is not carried.


[1] The model could not represent the baseline it was being asked to beat
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: held-out RMSE on gr1.PickNPlace — constant velocity 0.02717, learned model
             0.05900. The model lost to a predictor that ignores the action entirely,
             and lost to identity too once early stopping was added.

Cause:       `z` carried position only. Constant-velocity motion is a function of the
             PREVIOUS state, which the latent did not contain, so no amount of training
             could express it. The model was being scored against a hypothesis it was
             structurally blind to.

Partial fix that did NOT work: adding velocity to the latent ([pos | vel], robot 44d ->
             88d). Held-out 0.05795 — still worse than identity. The model now had the
             information but was initialised at the IDENTITY, so it had to learn its way
             to constant velocity before learning anything about the action, and on
             1,760 samples from 4 episodes it spent its whole budget doing that.

Fix:         `integrate_velocity` — the base prediction carries velocity into position,
             so the UNTRAINED model is exactly the constant-velocity predictor and the
             learned term only supplies the action's correction. Same principle as the
             motion gate one level up: start at the strongest known predictor.

Metric before/after (held-out test episode, 3 train / 1 val / 1 test):
             identity          0.05540
             constant velocity 0.02615
             model, before     0.05900   (worse than both)
             model, after      0.0202-0.0258 across 3 seeds

Caveat kept: the margin over constant velocity ranges +1.2% to +22.6% by seed. Three
             training episodes cannot distinguish a learned dynamics from a lucky init,
             and RESULTS.md reports the range rather than the best run.


[2] Early stopping was selecting on the reported test set
-----------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: validation error bottomed at epoch 15 and rose after, so the best epoch was
             being restored — using the same episode the final number was reported on.

Cause:       A two-way split. The "held-out" episode was doing double duty as model
             selection and as the measurement, which makes the reported figure an
             optimistic bound, not a measurement.

Fix:         Three-way split by episode: 3 train / 1 validation / 1 test. The epoch is
             chosen on validation; the test episode is touched once, at the end.


[3] The symmetry-breaking that destroyed the identity initialisation
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: the training script printed "model 0.07870 vs identity 0.06372 (equal by
             construction)". They were not equal, and the claim in the message was the
             thing that was wrong.

Cause:       `DynamicsEnsemble` perturbs member parameters at init so the members are
             not identical — without that, they agree about everything and the
             uncertainty signal is dead. The perturbation was applied to EVERY 2-D
             parameter including the output heads, whose zero initialisation is what
             makes the model start at the identity. Two deliberate properties, one
             silently cancelling the other.

Fix:         Perturb the trunk only; `slot_out`, `slot_gate` and `robot_out` stay
             exactly zero. Members still differ, and every member still starts by
             predicting "nothing changes". Verified: max |dz| = 0.00e+00 at init while
             member parameters differ.

Note:        an untrained ensemble now reports zero spread, which is correct — members
             that all predict no change genuinely agree. The earlier growing-uncertainty
             smoke test had been reading head noise as epistemic uncertainty.


[4] Collision cost that could not discriminate between candidates
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: stage 6.5 on the real scene vetoed all 8 candidates, each with an identical
             collision cost of 0.2400.

Cause:       The term scored ABSOLUTE predicted interpenetration. Mask3D's proposals on
             this scene already overlap — measured separately, all 15 instance pairs
             have overlapping boxes — so every candidate inherited the same pre-existing
             overlap and the term carried no information about any action. A planner
             choosing among actions cannot be charged for what is common to all of them.

Fix:         Score against the pre-action scene: collision and stability now charge only
             the INDUCED part, clamped at zero so a predicted improvement earns no bonus
             from a dynamics model this weak.

Metric before/after:  spurious vetoes on the real scene 8/8 -> 0/8.


[5] Uncertainty measured only part of the latent, so it was identically zero
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: every rollout of the trained proprioceptive checkpoint reported an
             uncertainty of exactly 0.00000 at every horizon, so the planner's risk
             term contributed nothing and never had.

Cause:       `DynamicsEnsemble.rollout` computed member disagreement over
             `slots[..., :6]` only. The trained model has NO object slots — a single
             dummy slot masked off — so `mask.sum()` was 0, the clamp made it 1, and the
             spread came out 0/1. A quantity measuring part of the latent treats the
             parts it skips as perfectly known.

Fix:         Combine slot-geometry spread and robot-state spread, averaging over the
             components that exist, so a scene with no slots is scored on the robot
             rather than on nothing.

Metric before/after (8-step rollout, held-out episode):
             reported spread at step 8:  0.00000  ->  0.14977


[6] The checkpoint recorded an architecture it could not rebuild
------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: `load_state_dict` failed with a 64x88 vs 64x44 size mismatch on every
             member when anything tried to reconstruct the model from the checkpoint.

Cause:       `train.py` saved `robot_dim` as the raw state width (44). With velocity
             carried the latent is [position | velocity] and the model is built with 88.
             The checkpoint described a model that was never trained.

Fix:         Save the dims the model was BUILT with, plus `integrate_velocity` and the
             normaliser statistics, and rebuild-and-load the model from the saved dict
             before writing it. A checkpoint that cannot reconstruct its own model is
             not a checkpoint, and the check belongs one line from its cause rather than
             in whatever consumer trips over it later.


[7] A headline number that was true and misleading
-----------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: training reported "+26.2% vs constant velocity" while an independent
             rollout evaluation reported the model LOSING to constant velocity by 37.7%
             at the same one-step horizon. Two of our own measurements disagreed.

Cause:       Neither was wrong. They aggregate differently and the data makes the
             choice decisive. Global RMSE squares before averaging and is dominated by
             the worst samples; mean/median per-sample error describes the typical one.
             Constant velocity beats the model on 91% of samples and is 4.6x better on
             the median, while the model is much better in the tail (p99 0.083 vs 0.152;
             on constant velocity's ten worst samples, 40% better).

             Physically this is what it should look like: constant velocity is excellent
             during smooth motion and fails at direction changes and contact, which is
             exactly where the action matters.

Fix:         Report both aggregations plus the win rate, everywhere. The one-line claim
             was arithmetically correct and gave the opposite impression of the typical
             case, which is the kind of number that survives review and then fails in
             use.

Lesson kept: a single aggregate over a skewed error distribution is a choice about
             which samples matter, not a neutral summary. Say which one, or report both.


[8] A track that looked healthy and was on the wrong object
-------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: the extracted "orange plastic bowl" track reported 99.5% visibility and a
             travel of 0.811 normalised image units. The bowl never moves.

Cause:       instances were assigned by CLIP label argmax alone. The robot arm is
             orange, CLIP scores it happily as "an orange bowl", and every time the arm
             swung past a keyframe it took over the bowl's identity. Every summary
             statistic looked healthy; only drawing the track on the frames showed it.

Fix:         associate on label AND position — a keyframe re-anchor must land near where
             the track already is, the same reason `InstanceRegistry` associates by IoU
             in 3-D. Objects do not teleport between observations.

Metric before/after (episode 0 bowl travel):  0.811 -> 0.131

Follow-on:   a `--check` flag renders the overlay, because the failure was invisible to
             every number the extractor produced.


[9] Optical flow followed the arm away and recorded it as object motion
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: in episode 2 the cube and bowl markers both ended up riding the retreating
             arm while the cube sat in the bowl.

Cause:       positions between SAM keyframes come from Lucas-Kanade flow, which follows
             whatever texture it latched onto. When the gripper occludes the cube, that
             texture is the gripper. The flow segment was recorded as measured data.

Fix:         trust a flow segment only when BRACKETED by two SAM confirmations, and mark
             everything else not-visible. The dataset then skips any transition whose
             frames were not all measured. A guess recorded as a measurement is how a
             dynamics model learns motion that never happened.

Effect:      the contaminated bowl track fell below the visibility threshold and was
             dropped from 4 of 5 episodes automatically. 295 of 2,343 transitions are
             now excluded as unobserved.


[10] Half the dataset was a demonstration where nothing happens
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: episode 3 is 1,797 frames — 43% of `cube_to_bowl_5` — with a cube travel of
             0.018 against 0.67-0.81 in every other episode.

Cause:       not a bug in the tracker. The tracks are correct; the robot simply never
             performs the task in that episode.

Fix:         `drop_idle_episodes`, thresholded on object travel inside a wide gap
             (0.070 idle against 0.67 working; the threshold is 0.20). Training on it
             would have made an identity predictor look unbeatable for a reason with
             nothing to do with dynamics.


[11] The train/test split drifted from the data it was splitting
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: `1193 train / 0 test transitions` at stride 30.

Cause:       split boundaries were computed arithmetically as
             `len(state) - 2*stride` per episode, in parallel with an index that was
             separately filtered for visibility. The two agreed until the filter started
             dropping transitions, after which train and test were misaligned — and at a
             long stride the test range came out empty, which is the only reason it was
             noticed. At stride 1 it silently mis-split.

Fix:         derive the split from the dataset's own index by episode membership, and
             fail loudly if any split is empty. Anything computed in parallel with the
             structure it describes will drift from it.

Consequence: every cube_to_bowl_5 number measured before this was recomputed. The
             corrected result moved the object pathway from "19% worse than identity" to
             "ties identity", which is a materially different conclusion.


[12] One velocity setting for two streams with opposite statistics
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-23
Fingerprint: with velocity integration on, the object pathway scored 19% WORSE than
             identity; the robot pathway needed it to beat identity at all.

Cause:       `integrate_velocity` made the untrained model a constant-velocity predictor
             for BOTH streams. For the robot that is the stronger baseline (0.0140
             against identity 0.0339). For the mask-derived object tracks it is the
             weaker one (0.1364 against identity 0.0975), because centroid jitter makes
             the slot velocity channel mostly noise. The object pathway was started from
             the worse of the two predictors.

Fix:         choose it per stream (`integrate_slot_velocity`). Which baseline is stronger
             is a property of the signal, not of the architecture.

Metric before/after (objects vs identity):  -19.2%  ->  +0.1%


VERIFICATION STATUS
---------------------
VERIFIED (CPU, tiny inputs, 32 tests):
  - untrained model is exactly the identity, and exactly constant-velocity with
    `integrate_velocity`;
  - padded slots never influence a prediction or a score, and padding does not change
    the prediction for real slots;
  - ensemble uncertainty grows with rollout horizon;
  - collision and support terms match hand-computed values (0.5 m^3, 1.95 m);
  - the planner vetoes an action-induced collision and ignores pre-existing overlap.

MEASURED ON REAL DATA:
  - proprioceptive dynamics beats identity and constant velocity on a held-out episode,
    with the seed variance recorded above.

NOT VALIDATED:
  - per-candidate uncertainty. The ensemble spread tracks how error grows with HORIZON
    (14.1x predicted against 14.2x realised over 8 steps) but has rank correlation
    -0.071 against realised per-rollout error, so it cannot say which candidate is
    riskier. The risk weight defaults to zero because of this.
  - object dynamics. Per-instance tracks now exist for all five cube_to_bowl_5
    episodes (extract_tracks.py) and are verified correct, but the model ties an
    identity predictor at every step size tested and its best validation score is at
    epoch 0. Cause measured: median single-step displacement 0.00084 (half a pixel),
    action R^2 = 0.016 at one frame rising to 0.139 at one second, on two training
    episodes. The object pathway is architecture, not a result, and nothing downstream
    should treat its predictions as informative.
  - (closed) multi-step rollout error is now measured over 8 steps on 319 held-out
    windows; see RESULTS.md. The model beats constant velocity at 8/8 horizons on global
    RMSE and 0/8 on the median sample.

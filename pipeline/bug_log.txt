Pipeline glue — bug log
========================
Scope: the handoffs between stages. RGB-D -> SLAM -> scene export -> OpenMask3D ->
FoundationPose -> ROS2. Every bug here is geometric and silent: nothing crashes, the
wrong thing is simply described with full confidence.


[1] open3d 0.20.0 ScalableTSDFVolume integrates nothing on arm64 macOS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-21
Fingerprint: `fuse_tsdf` returned an empty point cloud for input that is obviously
             valid. Narrowed with a direct probe:

               create_from_rgbd_image           -> 76800 points, mean z = 1.5  (correct)
               ScalableTSDFVolume.extract_point_cloud       -> 0
               ScalableTSDFVolume.extract_voxel_point_cloud -> 0
               UniformTSDFVolume.extract_voxel_point_cloud  -> 58525
               UniformTSDFVolume.extract_point_cloud        -> 11956

             `extract_voxel_point_cloud` returning 0 is what localised it: that reads
             raw voxels, so the failure is in `integrate()`, not in surface extraction
             or normal estimation.

Ruled out along the way (each was a plausible theory, each was wrong):
  - lattice alignment. The first hypothesis was that a plane at exactly z=1.5 with
    voxel_length=0.02 puts the zero-crossing precisely on voxel corners. Testing
    z=1.51 gave 0 as well.
  - degenerate single view. Three views with a 5 cm baseline: still 0.
  - colour type. NoColor with convert_rgb_to_intensity=True: still 0.

Cause:       ScalableTSDFVolume.integrate() is broken in open3d 0.20.0 on this platform.
             It does not raise — it returns normally having written nothing.

Fix:         `fuse_tsdf` uses UniformTSDFVolume, with bounds derived from the camera
             track padded by the depth horizon and resolution capped at 512 (allocation
             is O(resolution^3)).

Why this one matters out of proportion to its size: an empty cloud is not an obviously
broken value. OpenMask3D would read it as "the room contains nothing", propose no
instances, and the pipeline would report a clean run with no objects found. The failure
would have looked like a segmentation problem, three stages downstream of the actual bug.


[2] Open3D integrates with WORLD-TO-CAMERA; our poses are CAMERA-TO-WORLD
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-21

`volume.integrate(rgbd, intrinsic, extrinsic)` wants the extrinsic, i.e. world-to-camera.
`slam_server.py` deliberately returns camera-to-world, because that is what
`nav_msgs/Odometry` wants (where the camera IS in the map frame) and what
`droid.terminate()` produces.

Passing ours straight through fuses a scene mirrored about the origin, which still looks
like a plausible room. `fuse_tsdf` inverts, and
test_fusion_places_geometry_in_front_of_the_camera pins it: a camera at the origin
looking down +z at a 1.5 m wall must yield points near z = +1.5, not -1.5.

This is the same convention hazard as the DROID pose direction and the scalar-last
quaternion. Three different places in this pipeline where a sign or an inversion is
invisible in the output's shape.


NOTE — the gauge, and why SceneWriter refuses without it
----------------------------------------------------------
Monocular DROID normalises disparity (`depth_video.normalize`), so its translations
carry an arbitrary scale. Fusing those gives a scene whose size is wrong by an unknown
factor — and since the per-instance mesh cut from that scene is what FoundationPose
registers against, the 6-DoF pose and the pre-grasp offset inherit the error.

Nothing downstream can detect it: a room scaled by 0.7 is a perfectly consistent room.
So `SceneWriter` refuses a non-metric trajectory at the boundary rather than letting it
propagate, and `slam_server.py` reports `metric` so the boundary has something to check.

[S1] A world-welded base geom silently locked the arm's yaw axis
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-27
Fingerprint: The scripted demonstrator reached, grasped and lifted the cube correctly,
             then stalled in `transfer` for the whole episode, 0/10 success. The trace
             was the tell:

               commanded target yaw  -0.577 -> +1.200 (ran to the joint limit)
               actual tip yaw        -0.577 -> -0.577 (never moved at all)
               tip radius            0.350  ->  0.439 (followed the command fine)

             So shoulder, elbow and wrist tracked their targets and base_yaw did not.
             `data.actuator_force[0]` sat pinned at 40 N·m — its forcerange — with zero
             velocity, which rules out a wiring or indexing error: the actuator was
             pushing as hard as it could against something.

Ruled out:   actuator/joint name collision; wrong `data.ctrl` index (verified 0..5 map
             to the six actuators); joint limits (range ±1.2, position -0.58); damping
             and armature (at 40 N·m and damping 4 it should spin at 10 rad/s).

Cause:       `mj_forward` reported four permanent contacts, `pedestal <-> yaw_link`,
             each at dist = -0.02 — the two coaxial base cylinders overlapped by 2 cm.
             MuJoCo's parent-child contact filter did not exclude them because the
             `pedestal` body carries no joint, so the compiler WELDS it to the world;
             the exemption for a parent and its child does not apply when the parent is
             the world body. Four deeply penetrating contacts between coaxial cylinders
             produce almost no radial force and an enormous friction torque about the
             shared axis — which is exactly the one axis that stopped moving.

Fix:         Shortened the pedestal to end at z=0.26, where yaw_link begins, and added
             an explicit `<exclude body1="pedestal" body2="yaw_link"/>`. 0/10 -> 10/10
             canonical, 39/40 under domain randomisation.

Why it matters: nothing errored and nothing looked wrong in isolation. Three of four
joints worked, so every plausible hypothesis pointed at the fourth joint's own
configuration rather than at a contact pair a hundred lines away in the file.


[S2] CLIP's per-mask argmax discards a mask segmented at IoU 0.977
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-27
Fingerprint: Perception-derived reward agreed with simulator ground truth on only
             56.2% of episodes. Every single error was a false negative (fp=0, fn=14)
             and 13 of 14 read "not observed: an orange plastic bowl".

Ruled out:   SAM segmentation quality — this was the assumed cause and it was wrong.
             Dumping every mask against the ground-truth bowl showed SAM finds it at
             IoU 0.977-0.980 on the frames that "failed".
             Greedy assignment contention — the second hypothesis, also wrong. The bowl
             mask was not being stolen; it was scoring P(bowl) = 0.144 in its own right.
             Prompt wording — swept ten bowl phrasings against ground truth. The
             incumbent "an orange plastic bowl" (19/30 detections, mean IoU 0.781) was
             statistically tied with the best alternative, "an orange basket" (20/30).
             The vocabulary is not the lever.

Cause:       `label_masks` read the similarity matrix ACROSS ROWS — for each mask, its
             best prompt. CLIP's raw cosine similarities on these crops occupy a narrow
             0.21-0.32 band whatever the crop is, so that argmax is close to arbitrary:
             the correctly-segmented bowl's own best prompt came out "a small dark
             cube" (0.295) against the bowl's (0.289). One near-tie, and the object
             vanishes from the scene.

Fix:         Added `mask_text_similarity`, which returns the whole matrix, and read it
             DOWN ITS COLUMNS instead — for each label, the mask that best suits it —
             with a softmax at CLIP's logit scale so a threshold means something, and a
             greedy resolution so no mask serves two labels. 56.2% -> 62.5%.

Residual:    still 12 false negatives, all the same bowl. The remaining limit is CLIP's
             zero-shot discrimination on a small, low-texture synthetic object, not the
             assignment direction and not the prompt. Recorded rather than tuned away.
             The same weakness is latent in the real-data path — `extract_tracks.py`
             uses the row-wise read, and the earlier "arm stole the bowl's identity"
             bug there is the same failure with a different outcome.

Why it matters: the obvious diagnosis (the segmenter is weak) was wrong, and acting on
it would have meant replacing a component that was performing at IoU 0.98.

[S4] Relative improvement divided by an exactly-zero baseline
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-27
Fingerprint: ZeroDivisionError after the results had already printed; then, once
             guarded at 1e-9, a per-slot line reading "-34959.8%".
Cause:       Per-slot reporting prints `1 - model/identity`. The simulated bowl is a
             static body, so its identity error is exactly 0; the ball is never touched,
             so its identity error is tiny but nonzero. Real tracks always jitter, so
             neither case could arise on the data the code was written for.
Fix:         Guard at 1e-4 in standardised units. Below it the object is static, the
             ratio is meaningless, and what is reported instead is the absolute motion
             the model invents — which is the number that actually matters there.


[S5] The trained checkpoint could not be used, and nothing noticed
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Fingerprint: Found while wiring `loop.py`: `dynamics.pt` stores the state and action

Cause:       Slots are standardised during training, so a consumer feeding raw metres

[S7] The closed loop hovered over the cube for an entire episode
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-27
Fingerprint: 0/4 success in the first closed-loop run, every episode burning all 360
             steps. The arm reached the cube smoothly and correctly and then simply
             stayed there. Nothing errored, no action was malformed, and the perception
             error was a healthy 1.24 cm throughout.
Cause:       `scripted_chunk` saves and restores the waypoint machine's stage around
             its lookahead — correctly, since interrogating a policy about the next
             eight steps must not advance it eight steps. But the loop then executed
             the PLANNED chunk without ever calling `pol.act` on a real observation, so
             the only thing that ever touched the machine was the lookahead, and the
             lookahead always put it back. Stage 0 forever.
Fix:         Advance the machine once per control step on the real observation and
             discard its action; the executed action still comes from the planner.
             Driving the stage from reality rather than from the dead-reckoned chunk
             also means a grasp that slips is noticed, instead of the machine
             proceeding on the assumption that its own plan worked.
Effect:      0/4 -> 6/6, then 24/24 at zero policy noise.
Guarded by:  two tests that have to disagree — `test_lookahead_does_not_advance_the_
             policy` (the restore is correct) and `test_the_waypoint_machine_advances_
             during_a_run` (something else must do the advancing). Either alone would
             have passed while the loop was broken.


[S8] "Planning hurts" was confounded until a third arm was added
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-27
Fingerprint: Not a crash — a conclusion that was not yet earned. The plan/no-plan grid
             showed planning losing at every noise level (100% -> 66.7% at noise 0.004)
             and losing more the more it intervened, which reads as a clean indictment
             of the scorer.
Cause:       The two arms did not differ only in ranking. `perturbed_candidates` builds
             the candidate set by adding Cartesian jitter to the policy's chunk, so the
             planning arm also absorbs noise the no-plan arm never sees. The comparison
             therefore measures (ranking quality) + (cost of the perturbation), and
             attributes the sum to the scorer.
Fix:         Added `--pick {best,random,first}` and ran a random-pick arm over the SAME
             candidate set. At noise 0.004: first 100.0%, random 54.2%, best 66.7%.
Conclusion:  Both effects are real and opposite. The scorer ranks better than chance
             (+12.5 points, and it removes the unsafe events) — the world model is
             doing real work. The candidate set costs 46 points, and ranking wins back
             only 12. So planning is net negative *as configured*, and the thing to fix
             is the candidate source, not the scorer: `perturbed_candidates` documents
             itself as a stand-in for sampling the policy K times, and additive noise
             makes every candidate but one strictly worse than the policy's own answer.

Why this one is in the log at all: the unconfounded result and the confounded one point
at different repairs. Acting on the first table would have meant rebuilding the scorer,
which is the component that was working.


[S9] Every observation mixed two timesteps
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-27
Fingerprint: A snapshot/restore test failed on `tip` — and only on `tip` — by 4.3e-4 m,
             while qpos, qvel, ctrl and the commanded target all restored exactly. The
             restored value was the *correct* one, which is what made it confusing.
Cause:       `mj_step` integrates qpos but leaves the derived frames — `xpos`,
             `geom_xpos`, `xmat` — describing the configuration it started from; they
             are recomputed at the top of the next forward pass. So `state` (read from
             qpos) was current while `tip` and `objects` (read from xpos) were one step
             behind. Restoring and calling `mj_forward` produced fresh frames, and the
             "drift" was the staleness being removed.
Impact:      0.43 mm on the tip, against ~12 mm of motion per step — 3.6%. Small, but
             it sat between a dynamics model's input and its target, and between the
             policy and the tip it was servoing to.
Fix:         `mj_forward` at the end of `step()`, before observing. One forward pass.
Why it hid:  nothing is inconsistent within either timestep, and both are plausible. A
             test comparing the env against itself would never catch it; this only
             surfaced because a rewind produced a *different* answer than the forward
             path for the same qpos.


[S10] Oracle@k perturbed the episode it was measuring
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-28
Fingerprint: Two grid cells that must be identical were not. `belief/first` scored
             15/16 where `action/first` scored 16/16 — but `first` executes the
             unperturbed policy chunk in both, so the candidate source cannot matter.
             The only difference between the runs was the diagnostic rollouts, which
             are supposed to leave no trace whatsoever.
Cause:       `snapshot` saved a hand-picked set of fields — qpos, qvel, act, ctrl,
             time. That list looks complete and omits `qacc_warmstart`, the constraint
             solver's warm start. A contact-rich scene resumed without it takes a
             different first solver iteration; the difference is far below a micron and
             compounds chaotically over 200 steps.
Fix:         `mj_getState`/`mj_setState` with `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION` — MuJoCo's own
             definition of what integration needs — instead of a hand-picked list.
Guarded by:  a replay test that runs 60 steps THROUGH A GRASP after a detour that also
             makes contact, and demands agreement at 1e-12. The earlier version replayed
             15 contact-free steps and passed with the bug present, which is the whole
             lesson: a rewind test that avoids contact tests nothing about rewinding.
Why it matters out of proportion to its size: this is the instrument, not the subject.
A diagnostic that changes the thing it measures produces numbers that look fine and
cannot be checked against anything.


[S11] The "bowl" is not a bowl, and the perception result was mostly that
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-28
Fingerprint: SAM+CLIP reward agreement stuck at 62.5%, every error a false negative and
             every false negative the bowl. [S2] fixed the assignment direction and a
             ten-prompt sweep moved it by one frame in thirty, so the remaining gap was
             recorded as "CLIP's zero-shot discrimination on a small low-texture
             synthetic object".

             That explanation was wrong. Replacing CLIP with an open-vocabulary
             DETECTOR (OWLv2) — which should have been strictly better at localising
             from text — made the bowl *worse*: best query 4/24 detections against
             CLIP's 19/30. Meanwhile the same detector found the cube at 0.599 and the
             ball at 0.874 in the same frames.

             That split is the clue. A cube is a cube and a sphere is a sphere; both are
             canonical. The bowl is eight upright boxes in a ring.

Cause:       A query sweep with shape-honest phrasings settles it:

               "an orange plastic bowl"     mean 0.003   0/12   boxIoU 0.307
               "an orange ring"             mean 0.009   0/12   boxIoU 0.111
               "a ring of orange blocks"    mean 0.460  11/12   boxIoU 0.653

             The detector is not failing. It is correctly describing what is in the
             image, and the image does not contain a bowl. CLIP scored *better* only
             because it is a fuzzier matcher — being vaguer made it more forgiving of a
             label that does not fit.

Conclusion:  the perception gap was largely an ARENA modelling error, not a perception
             stack weakness, and three rounds of work on the perception stack could not
             have found it. What found it was a component that was more correct.

Fix:         Rebuilt the bowl as 12 wall segments flared 30 degrees from a 58 mm base
             to an 88 mm rim (quaternions generated by scratchpad/genbowl.py; euler
             order is ambiguous enough to be worth avoiding for a compound rotation).
             No perception code was touched.

               OWLv2, "an orange plastic bowl"   before 0.003, 0/12
                                                  after 0.367, 9/12
               OWLv2, "an orange bowl"            after 0.424, 10/12

             A 100x score increase from reshaping the object. The demonstrator is
             unaffected (39/40 under randomisation), which is the check that the fix
             changed the appearance and not the task.

             Recorded before the fix as well as after, so the sequence is not rewritten
             afterwards as though the geometry had always been the suspect. It was the
             third hypothesis, and the first two were both wrong.


[S12] The lookahead spent the policy's own randomness
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-28
Fingerprint: Two grid cells that are identical by construction disagreed — `action/first`
             16/16 against `belief/first` 15/16. Both execute the unperturbed policy
             chunk, so the candidate source cannot reach the executed action. [S10] had
             already fixed one cause of exactly this symptom (an inexact rewind), and
             the disagreement survived it.
Cause:       `scripted_chunk` restores the waypoint machine's stage but not its RNG.
             With action noise on, `act` draws from that RNG on every call, so a
             lookahead of H steps consumes H draws — and the belief source calls the
             lookahead K times per decision against the action source's once. The two
             arms were therefore running different noise realisations of the same
             policy.
Fix:         Save and restore `bit_generator.state` alongside the stage. This also makes
             belief candidates differ by BELIEF alone, which is the only thing they were
             supposed to vary.
Guarded by:  a test asserting the generator does not advance across five lookaheads and
             does advance when the policy acts for real. Both halves matter: freezing it
             entirely would make the action noise deterministic and silently disable the
             thing the noise was added to simulate.

Why it recurs in this log: three separate bugs ([S10], [S12], and the frozen stage in
[S7]) are all the same mistake — deciding what "the state" is by listing the fields that
came to mind. MuJoCo answers it properly with `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION`; for our own objects
nothing does, so the rule now is that anything a lookahead touches gets saved, RNGs
included.


[S13] 377 of 380 successful episodes were labelled failures
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-28
Fingerprint: `train_value.py` opened with "380 episodes, 3 successful". The collector
             only WRITES successful episodes, so the correct figure is 380 of 380. The
             value head had therefore been fitted to three positive examples in sixty
             thousand frames — and still reported beating its baselines by ~10%, because
             predicting approximately zero everywhere is an excellent fit to a column
             that is approximately always zero.
Cause:       `next.reward` fired on the POLICY reaching its terminal waypoint stage
             ("done"), not on the SIMULATOR reporting success. `env.done()` ends the
             episode the moment `success()` is true, which happens at stage "release",
             so the machine reaches "done" in roughly 1 episode in 40. The two
             definitions of "finished" disagreed and the wrong one was recorded.
Fix:         `reward_column` fires 1.0 on the first frame `env.success()` is true, from
             a per-frame `solved` flag recorded during the rollout. 3/380 -> 380/380.
             Fires on the FIRST such frame rather than the last, so a discounted
             return-to-go does not credit the idle tail after the cube has landed.
Note:        `train_value.py`'s docstring already claimed the label "comes from the same
             simulator verdict the loop is scored against rather than from a heuristic
             applied here". That was the intent and it was not true of the data. The
             fix makes the sentence accurate rather than deleting it.

Why it survived a review: the column existed, was per-frame, had the right dtype and the
right shape, and was multiplied by an episode-level success flag that was itself
correct. Everything about it looked right except how often it was nonzero, and nothing
downstream read it until the value head did.


[S14] An AUC of 0.764 computed from a column of zeros
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-28
Fingerprint: A signal-selection table reported predicted collision volume at AUC 0.764
             — comfortably the best of five candidate disaster signals, and the basis
             for redesigning the veto around it. The threshold sweep then fired on zero
             decisions at every quantile from 0.40 to 0.99, which is impossible for a
             signal carrying that much information.
Cause:       The Mann-Whitney AUC broke ties by `np.argsort` order instead of averaging
             them. Collision was identically **0.0 on all 164 decisions**, so the
             "ranking" was whatever arbitrary order argsort produced, and where the
             positives happened to land in it decided the score. The function
             manufactured a strong predictor out of a column with no variance at all.
Fix:         Average ranks within ties, which scores a constant signal at exactly 0.500,
             and print an explicit "(constant — carries no information by construction)"
             note when a candidate signal has zero range, so the degenerate case is
             named rather than silently scored.
Effect:      collision 0.764 -> 0.500. Corrected table: drop 0.353, v_end 0.307,
             collision 0.500, instability 0.411, spread 0.483 — no signal predicts
             disaster and three are anti-predictive.

Why collision was constant is the more interesting half, and it is structural rather
than a bug: the latent carries the cube, the bowl and the ball, and **no gripper**. The
collision term can therefore only see object-object overlap, which does not occur in an
8-step horizon during normal operation — while the actual failures in this task are
gripper-object events. The model cannot represent the thing that goes wrong.

This is the fourth instrument in this sequence to be wrong in the flattering direction
([S10], [S12], [S13], [S14]). Three were caught by cells that should have been identical
not being identical; this one by a downstream result contradicting an upstream one. The
pattern is that a measurement which agrees with what you hoped deserves the same
scepticism as one that does not.


[S15] The fix that was right in simulation was wrong on the real data
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Date:        2026-09-28
Fingerprint: [S2] established — in the simulator, against ground truth — that reading
             the CLIP similarity matrix down its COLUMNS beats reading it across its
             rows: SAM segments the bowl at IoU 0.977 and the row-wise argmax of that
             exact mask is "a small dark cube". Applying the same change to the real
             extraction regressed it. The bowl is static and its track travelled:

               row-wise      bowl found in 1 of 5 episodes; travel 0.070 when found
               column-wise   bowl found in 5 of 5; travel 0.180 / 0.680 / 0.068 / 0.734

Cause:       The two datasets fail differently and the column read only helps one of
             them. In simulation the label competing for the bowl's mask is "a small
             dark cube" — another object we want — so considering every mask for every
             wanted label is exactly right. On the real video the competitor is the
             **arm**, which is orange, and which we do not want at all; asking "which
             mask best suits 'an orange plastic bowl'" hands it the arm.

Attempted:   rejecting a mask for a wanted label when an unwanted label dominates it.
             This helped episode 1 (0.233 -> 0.180) and did nothing for episodes 2 and
             4 (0.680, 0.734). CLIP does not reliably rate an arm crop as more arm than
             bowl, so the domination test has nothing to fire on.

Resolution:  `--label-read` is now an explicit flag, defaulting to **row** for the real
             extraction and **column** in `sim/perception.py`, with the measurement
             recorded on the flag itself. Not a fix — a documented, measured divergence.
             Shipping the column read everywhere would have improved a benchmark and
             degraded the data the project is actually about.

Still open:  neither read produces a usable real-data bowl track. Row-wise loses it;
             column-wise finds the arm. The identified fix is the one
             `world_model/reward.py` already uses for exactly this object and exactly
             this reason — **detect the bowl once, by median over probe frames, and
             treat it as static rather than tracking it.** A static object does not need
             a track, and tracking it is what exposes it to being stolen.

The general lesson, which cost a full re-extraction to learn: a fix validated on the
dataset with ground truth is a hypothesis about the dataset without it, not a result
there. Verifying on the real path is what caught this, and it is why the real path is
worth re-running even when the simulated evidence looks decisive.


[S16] Thirty launched cubes rewrote the normaliser, and every model downstream
Symptom:     the audit asked for a slot normaliser sanity check and got
             |slot_mean|max = 187.9114 metres from `world_model_plans`, on an arena
             whose table is 1.2 m wide. `world_model_sim`, trained on the same code
             the same day, read 0.2737.

Fingerprint: per-dataset max absolute object position
               pick_place_400b / 400c    0.399 m      0 episodes over 2 m
               plans_500                 9.6e+07 m    30 of 500 episodes over 2 m

Cause:       `--randomise-plans` samples grasp offsets on a ring, and an offset far
             enough off-centre pinches the cube at a corner between the pads. MuJoCo
             resolves the resulting interpenetration by ejecting it at enormous
             velocity. Nothing in the collector rejected the episode, and
             `--keep-failures` — added deliberately, so the dynamics model would see
             failures — kept every one of them.

             The damage is not that 6% of episodes are wrong. It is that the loss is
             standardised by a mean and std computed over all of them, so those 30
             episodes set the scale for the other 470: every real 12 mm cube motion
             became ~1e-4 in standardised units and contributed essentially no
             gradient.

Fix:         `SANE_POSITION_M = 2.0` in `collect.py` — an episode with any object
             beyond it is discarded at write time and counted in the run's meta, and
             `train.py` now prints the slot normaliser with an explicit alarm when it
             exceeds arena scale. Re-collected as `plans_600` (33 of 600 discarded).

Before/after (whole-latent held-out RMSE vs identity, and value vs the distance
             baseline; both on plan-randomised data):
               contaminated plans_500   +45.3% dynamics,  0.025241 value MSE
               clean plans_600          +38.8% dynamics,  0.013159 value MSE
             The dynamics number *fell*, which is the honest direction: the
             contaminated split's identity baseline was inflated by the same outliers.
             The value head improved 48%.

Invalidated: every number read off the `plans_500` checkpoint, specifically the veto
             AUC table and the plans_500 dynamics/value rows. NOT invalidated: anything
             computed from `pick_place_400b`/`400c`, and every Oracle@k, reachability,
             headroom, perception-F1 and rescue-rate figure, which are measured in the
             simulator or the segmenter and never touch a learned checkpoint.

Found by:    asking whether the conclusions came from the pipeline that actually loads
             the post-training checkpoint. The check that exposed it — print the
             normaliser and compare it to the physical scale of the scene — costs one

[S17] The cube was not launched by the grasp. It was launched by the contact solver
Symptom:     5.5% of plan-randomised episodes ended with an object past 2 m — and
             sometimes past 1e11 m. [S16] added a guard that discarded them; this is
             the root cause underneath that guard.

Fingerprint: one step before the end, |v| of the cube goes from 0.7 m/s to 83 m/s with
             no change in the commanded action. For several steps before that the cube
             is 4-5 mm inside the table, pushed there by a finger pad while the arm's
             position servo keeps driving down.

First hypothesis, REFUTED: off-centre grasps pinch the cube at a corner, so the fix is
             a tighter `graspable_radius`. Measured over 300 episodes by decomposing
             each grasp offset into the gripper's own frame (radial = along the pad,
             tangential = along the closing axis):
               blow-up rate vs |tangential|  7.9 / 5.0 / 4.7 / 5.4 / 3.3 / 0.0 / 4.8 %
               blow-up rate vs |radial|      2.9 / 4.8 / 0.0 / 5.0 / 2.4 / 4.3 / 6.0 %
             Flat in both, and the smallest blowing-up offset was 0.44 mm. The plan is
             not what distinguishes the episodes that explode. Hypothesis dropped
             rather than shipped.

Cause:       the free objects carried `solref="0.006 1"` — a 6 ms contact time constant
             against MuJoCo's 0.02 default, i.e. deliberately *stiffer* than standard —
             with `solimp` width 1 mm. Penetrations of 4-11 mm are far past that width,
             so the solver applies its maximum restoring impulse to a 0.5 kg cube in a
             2 ms step. Deep penetration is routine here (it happens in nearly every
             episode) because the arm's position actuators are strong; what was rare
             was the impulse resolving into an ejection rather than a bounce.

Fix:         `solref="0.03 1"` on every geom a free object can strike — cube, ball,
             table, bowl base and bowl walls. The finger pads keep 0.006, since that
             value was chosen to stop a rigid pinch jittering and is load-bearing for
             the grasp.

Before/after (200 plan-randomised episodes per row):
               baseline  solref 0.006/0.02   11/200 blow-ups (5.5%)   success 75.5%
               solref 0.03 on free objects    2/200            (1.0%)          74.5%
               solref 0.03 on ALL contacts    0/200            (0.0%)          77.5%
             The 0/200 did not hold up at scale. At **2000** episodes the shipped
             configuration blows up 10 times — **0.5%**, an 11x reduction rather than
             an elimination, and the SANE_POSITION_M guard stays load-bearing.
             Success went UP,
             so this is not a stability-for-fidelity trade. Rejected alternatives:
             `solver iterations 200` changed nothing (5.5%, reverted per the Debug
             Rule); `timestep 0.001` removed the blow-ups and destroyed the task
             (success 75.5% -> 31.0%), because the waypoint machine's dwell counts are
             in steps, not seconds.

Check:       the demonstrator is unaffected — 10/10 canonical, 40/40 under full domain
             randomisation (was 39/40). That is the test that separates "changed the
             numerics" from "changed the task".

Interaction: `--keep-failures` exists so the dynamics sees failures, and it was keeping
             these. It now cannot: the physics check runs before it, and the two counts
             are reported separately. A task failure is data; a physics failure is a
             number that sets the normaliser for every other episode.


[S18] A leave-one-out AUC that inverted its own signal
Symptom:     asked whether ensemble spread and action deviation are better together
             than alone, the leave-one-episode-out evaluation returned AUC **0.188** for
             spread — a strong BACKWARDS predictor — where the same signal scores 0.528
             in sample. Every combination came back below 0.5 as well.

Cause:       the per-fold logistic scores were **pooled into one AUC**. Each fold fits
             its own intercept, and because the fold excludes exactly one episode, that
             intercept anti-correlates with the held-out episode's own failure rate:
             folds that drop a doomed episode see a healthier training set and emit
             lower logits for it. Pooling then orders decisions by which fold produced
             them rather than by the signal, and the ordering is backwards. The scores
             were never on a common scale, so ranking them together was meaningless.

Fix:         compute the AUC **within** each held-out episode and average by pair count,
             so no two scores from different folds are ever compared. Episodes with no
             failure or no success contribute no pair and are skipped.

Before/after:
                                      pooled LOEO     per-episode LOEO
               spread                    0.188             0.516
               action_dev                0.329             0.508
               spread + action_dev       0.314             0.476

Consequence: the corrected numbers change the conclusion rather than tidying it. Within
             an episode — which is where a veto actually decides — every signal is at
             chance (0.506-0.516), and combining two does not help (0.476). The pooled
             AUCs of 0.528-0.587 reported earlier were measuring **between-episode**
             discrimination: some episodes are doomed overall and run at higher spread.
             That is not a usable veto signal, because the gate is not asked "is this a
             bad episode", it is asked "is this the decision to refuse". It also
             explains, consistently, why the closed-loop veto ablation showed no gain
             (79.2% vs 80.8% over 120 episodes) and why every bootstrap CI on the net
             benefit includes zero.

Second time: this is the same class of bug as [S14], where AUC ties broken by sort order
             manufactured a 0.764 predictor out of a column of zeros. Both times the
             instrument produced a strong result from no data, and both times the tell
             was a number too good for the signal it came from.


[S19] A completion marker that fired on termination, not on completion
Symptom:     a 20-minute measurement was reported as finished four seconds after it
             launched, and the waiting job that depended on it woke up and reported
             "done" with no result. Asked for the number, there was none: the run had
             died on an argparse error in under a second.

Cause:       two mistakes that only bite together.

             1. The script ended with an unconditional `echo VETO_GOAL_DONE`. That
                marker says "this script reached its last line", which is not the same
                statement as "the work succeeded", and the two diverge exactly when it
                matters.
             2. `set -e` did not catch it, because the command was piped:
                    conda run ... python -m pipeline.sim.calibrate_veto ... | grep -v ...
                A pipeline's exit status is its LAST command's, so `grep` succeeding
                masked the Python process exiting 2. Without `set -o pipefail` the
                shell never saw a failure at all.

             The underlying defect was mine as well: `--signal fail_logit` was added to
             the recorded columns but never to the argparse `choices`, so the run could
             not have started.

Fix:         the marker is now emitted only on success, `pipefail` is set so a failure
             inside a pipeline is visible, and a failure emits its own distinct marker
             so a waiter can tell "finished" from "died":

                 set -euo pipefail
                 if <command> | <filter>; then echo TAG_DONE; else echo TAG_FAILED; fi

Rule:        **a completion marker must be bound to the completion of the work, never
             to the start or the end of the process that was supposed to do it.**
             A marker that fires on launch ("started"), on reaching the last line, or on
             any step other than the work's own success is an instrument that reports
             its own execution rather than the experiment's. The same rule is why
             `next.reward` had to fire on the simulator's success flag rather than on
             the policy reaching its terminal stage ([S13]): a proxy for completion
             drifts from completion precisely in the cases worth knowing about.

Consequence: the measurement itself, once actually run, is in RESULTS.md — a head
             trained on the failure label scores pooled AUC 0.563 and **within-episode
             AUC 0.499** against per-decision rewind ground truth, i.e. exactly chance
             where the gate decides. The wasted time was a few minutes; the risk was
             reporting "no result" as "no signal", which would have been a fourth
             withdrawn claim.



[S20] A deadlock fix that silently redefined the task
Symptom:     closed-loop success at K=32, same flags, same seeds, same checkpoint:
                 before   37.5%  [16.7, 58.3]   n=24
                 after    79.2%  [66.7, 89.6]   n=48
             Non-overlapping intervals for what was recorded as the same experiment.

Cause:       `ScriptedPickPlace.stage_timeout` was added between the two runs, to break
             a deadlock in phase-aligned commitment: an off-centre `descend` target sits
             inside the cube's own footprint, the gripper cannot reach it, the tip never
             comes within `tolerance`, and the stage never ends.

             It was introduced as a robustness parameter and it is not one. Before it, a
             stalled stage consumed the rest of the episode; after it, the machine moves
             on after 120 steps. That is a different task — and it changes fixed-K
             selection, which was never the reason for adding it, because a longer
             commitment keeps the arm away from the nominal waypoint for longer and so
             stalls more often.

             The nominal demonstrator is genuinely unaffected (10/10 canonical, 40/40
             randomised, p90 stage 53 steps against a 120-step cap), which is exactly
             what made the change look safe. The check that passed was the check for the
             wrong thing: it verified the DEMONSTRATOR was unchanged, not that every
             consumer of the demonstrator was.

Fix:         `loop.task_fingerprint()` hashes everything that defines the task — the
             demonstrator's speed and dwell constants, the waypoint heights, the arm
             geometry and the arena XML — and every run records it and prints it. Two
             numbers are comparable only if their fingerprints match, which is now a
             property of the files rather than of anyone's memory of when a constant
             moved.

Rule:        **a constant that changes what the robot does is part of the task
             definition, not a parameter.** Changing one invalidates every prior
             measurement that could depend on it, and "could depend on it" is much wider
             than the reason the change was made. Results from either side of such a
             change must be re-run or marked NOT COMPARABLE; they may never be put in
             the same table.

Invalidated: every closed-loop result driven by affordance candidates and measured
             before this change — the whole commitment sweep, the Oracle@k reachability
             figures, the domain-gate ablation, the plan-thrash trace, and the first
             recoverability run. Re-running them under one fingerprint; anything not
             re-run is marked in RESULTS.md rather than quietly retained.


[S21] The comparability check certified two different tasks as the same one
Symptom:     the re-run K sweep, built specifically to put the whole curve under one
             task definition after [S20], disagreed with itself:

                 K=16, 48 episodes, oracle selection, same checkpoint, same seeds
                     18.8%  [ 8.3, 31.2]
                     83.3%  [72.9, 93.8]

             Both runs printed `task 3de92c99cbe5 — comparable only with runs carrying
             the same fingerprint`.

Cause:       the two runs differed by `--max-env-steps` (360 vs 480), and
             `task_fingerprint()` did not read it. The budget was treated as an
             experiment parameter because it is a CLI flag rather than a module
             constant, which is a distinction about where a value is typed, not about
             what it means. How many steps the task gives you is the task.

             The effect is as large as it is because every failure in this arena is a
             timeout — 100% of failed episodes at K=16/32/64 and both phase modes end
             at exactly the step cap, and successful K=16 episodes finish at a median
             of 335 steps against a 360 cap. At that margin the budget is not a bound
             on the measurement, it IS the measurement.

Fix:         `max_env_steps` is hashed with the rest of the task. The specific error was
             mine and so was the general one: [S20] built this instrument, and then I
             fed the new sweep a flag the instrument could not see.

Rule:        **a comparability check that omits a field does not fail silently — it
             certifies the wrong answer.** An unchecked constant leaves you uncertain;
             an incomplete check that prints a matching hash removes the uncertainty in
             the wrong direction, and is worse than no check at all. When adding a
             fingerprint, enumerate the inputs of the thing being fingerprinted rather
             than the constants that happen to live nearby — every CLI flag is a
             candidate, and the test is "does this change what the robot must do",
             never "is this defined in a module".

Invalidated: the 480-step cells of the re-run K sweep (K=1, 2, 4, 8, 16). Re-run at the
             360-step budget every other post-[S20] cell already uses.

[S23] Two stages of a cascade composed by multiplying their AUCs
Symptom:     the run-time pivotal proxy was reported as too weak to gate on, in these
             words: "a positive effect that currently requires an oracle to locate."
             The basis was its standalone AUC against the true pivotal label —
             0.572, later 0.657 for the short-horizon variant — judged against the
             return-drop stage it would feed.

             Measured as an actual two-stage gate on the same decisions, it is not
             weak at all:

                 drop alone,        316 fires    +3.2 / 100   [-1.3, +7.9]
                 proxy -> drop,     158 fires   +12.0 / 100   [+4.8, +19.9]
                 oracle pivotal,    157 fires   +17.2 / 100   [+6.2, +26.7]

             The proxy recovers about 70% of what a perfect pivotal detector would
             buy, at the same firing rate, and its interval excludes zero where the
             single-stage gate's does not.

Cause:       AUC is a ranking summary over a whole population. A cascade is a
             CONJUNCTION of thresholds evaluated on the same rows, and its yield
             depends on how the two signals co-vary among the rows actually flagged —
             which no pair of marginal AUCs contains. Here the two stages are nearly
             independent (Spearman between proxy and drop: -0.101, -0.085, -0.018),
             so the conjunction filters far better than either margin suggests.

             The error runs in both directions and the mechanism is the same:

               * PESSIMISTIC, as above — two mediocre, independent signals compose
                 into a gate stronger than either, because each removes a different
                 part of the population the other gets wrong.
               * OPTIMISTIC — two strong but redundant signals compose into one
                 signal's worth of filtering. Measured here: `range [1/4 H]` and
                 `std [1/4 H]` score 0.657 and 0.655, and stacking them buys nothing,
                 because they are Spearman 0.995 with a Jaccard of 0.887 over the
                 top-30% flagged set. They are the same signal twice, and an AUC
                 product would have promised a second filter that does not exist.

Fix:         `pipeline/sim/analyse_gate.py` scores the combination end to end — net
             episode-outcome flips per 100 fires, bootstrapped over episodes — and
             prints the pairwise Spearman and flagged-set Jaccard alongside, so
             redundancy is visible in the same table as the yield. No composite is
             reported that was not measured as a composite.

Rule:        **a cascade must be measured as a cascade.** Per-stage ranking metrics
             do not compose: they carry no information about correlation between the
             stages, and correlation is the entire difference between two filters and
             one. The same applies to any figure of merit summarising a ranking rather
             than an operating point — AUC, Kendall tau, Spearman. When the deliverable
             is a decision, measure the decision: fires, rescues, breakages, and an
             interval over the unit that was resampled.

Consequence: a working run-time veto was declared unreachable and nearly abandoned on
             the strength of an arithmetic that was never run. This is the inverse of
             the failure in [S18] and [W4]: those reported a result the data did not
             support, this refused one the data did support. Both come from trusting a
             summary statistic in place of the measurement it summarises, and the
             honest direction of a claim is no guide to whether it needs checking.
